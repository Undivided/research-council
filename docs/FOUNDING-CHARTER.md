Research Council
A Multi-Agent Knowledge Continuity System
Vision and Architecture — Draft 2

1. The Core Idea
Research Council is an artificial intelligence framework designed around a simple premise:
Intelligence is not merely the ability to answer questions. Intelligence is the ability to maintain context, challenge assumptions, preserve knowledge, and improve decisions over time.
A single AI model can generate responses.
A council can generate understanding.
The Research Council architecture creates an environment where multiple specialized perspectives collaborate, challenge, refine, and preserve knowledge.
The goal is not to replace human judgment.
The goal is to amplify it.

2. The Fundamental Relationship
The human provides:
Purpose
The human defines:
    • What matters 
    • Why the question exists 
    • What outcomes are valuable 
    • What constraints are meaningful 
Without human purpose, intelligence becomes optimization without direction.

The council provides:
Perspective
Different agents examine the same problem through different lenses.
No single viewpoint is trusted completely.
Understanding emerges through interaction.

The evidence provides:
Constraint
Reality is the final authority.
Claims must survive contact with:
    • documented knowledge 
    • available data 
    • established facts 
    • reproducible reasoning 

3. The Council Members
The Researcher
Provides: Discovery
The researcher expands the knowledge space.
Responsibilities:
    • gather information 
    • identify relevant concepts 
    • explore possibilities 
    • find supporting evidence 
    • locate unanswered questions 
The researcher asks:
"What do we know?"

The Historian
Provides: Context
The historian prevents the council from forgetting what came before.
Responsibilities:
    • identify precedent 
    • compare historical patterns 
    • examine previous attempts 
    • understand evolution of ideas 
The historian asks:
"How did we get here?"

The Philosopher
Provides: Meaning
The philosopher examines the assumptions beneath the problem.
Responsibilities:
    • question definitions 
    • identify hidden assumptions 
    • examine ethical implications 
    • test conceptual foundations 
    • determine whether the question itself is properly framed 
The philosopher asks:
"Are we asking the right question?"
The philosopher prevents the council from becoming an efficient machine pursuing the wrong objective.

The Strategist
Provides: Direction
The strategist transforms understanding into action.
Responsibilities:
    • evaluate options 
    • identify opportunities 
    • prioritize approaches 
    • develop plans 
    • consider tradeoffs 
The strategist asks:
"Given what we know, what should we do?"

The Skeptic
Provides: Friction
The skeptic challenges confidence.
Responsibilities:
    • identify weak assumptions 
    • question conclusions 
    • locate unsupported claims 
    • expose reasoning errors 
The skeptic asks:
"Why might this be wrong?"

The Red Team
Provides: Adversarial Pressure
The red team attempts to break ideas before reality does.
Responsibilities:
    • simulate failure 
    • search for vulnerabilities 
    • identify unintended consequences 
    • challenge system integrity 
The red team asks:
"How could this fail?"

The Editor
Provides: Coherence
The editor transforms many perspectives into a unified product.
Responsibilities:
    • organize findings 
    • remove contradictions 
    • improve clarity 
    • create useful outputs 
The editor asks:
"Does this make sense?"

The Archivist
Provides: Memory
The archivist converts temporary reasoning into permanent knowledge.
Responsibilities:
    • identify durable insights 
    • create structured entries 
    • preserve discoveries 
    • maintain institutional memory 
The archivist asks:
"What should the council remember?"

The Librarian
Provides: Continuity
The librarian maintains access to accumulated knowledge.
Responsibilities:
    • organize stored information 
    • retrieve relevant context 
    • connect previous discoveries 
    • prevent repeated mistakes 
The librarian asks:
"What have we already learned?"

4. The Knowledge System
The Research Council does not rely on conversation memory alone.
Conversation is temporary.
Knowledge is permanent.
The system separates:
Working Memory
Temporary reasoning created during a council session.
Examples:
    • current analysis 
    • debate 
    • hypotheses 
    • drafts 

Institutional Memory
Validated knowledge preserved beyond a single session.
Examples:
    • research entries 
    • conclusions 
    • frameworks 
    • methodologies 
    • lessons learned 

The knowledge base provides:
Continuity
The council begins every new investigation with accumulated experience.
Each completed cycle improves future cycles.
The system becomes progressively more informed.

6. AI Model Independence
Research Council is not designed around a specific AI model.
The model is an interchangeable reasoning engine.
Possible future implementations:
    • local models 
    • cloud models 
    • specialized models 
    • hybrid systems 
The architecture separates:
Intelligence Provider
The AI model.
from:
Intelligence Framework
The council, workflow, memory, and validation system.
The model may change.
The institution remains.

7. Why Multiple Agents?
A single AI tends toward:
    • consistency 
    • agreement 
    • completion 
A council introduces:
    • disagreement 
    • correction 
    • competing viewpoints 
    • deliberate friction 
The purpose is not to create more answers.
The purpose is to create better decisions.

8. Design Principles
Principle 1 — No Single Perspective Is Sufficient
Complex problems require multiple viewpoints.

Principle 2 — Memory Creates Intelligence
An intelligence without memory repeats itself.

Principle 3 — Challenge Improves Reliability
Agreement is easy.
Correction is valuable.

Principle 4 — Structure Beats Raw Capability
A moderate model inside a strong framework can outperform a stronger model without structure.

Principle 5 — Humans Remain the Source of Purpose
The council provides reasoning.
The human provides meaning.

The goal is not to build an AI that knows everything.
